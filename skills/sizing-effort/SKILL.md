# Sizing effort

A size is how much of the user's own time a task takes, the way they usually work: getting ready,
doing it and checking the result. It is not how long an agent works on it.

Put a task on the size whose examples it is closest to, and on the larger one when in doubt. The
times are rough marks, far apart on purpose: compare the task with the examples, never work out
its hours.

- Cannot tell, for example because you do not know whether the user or an agent will do the work:
  leave the size empty and tell the user why in one sentence. An empty size is better than a
  guess.
- An agent does most of the work: size what is left for the user, such as explaining the task and
  reviewing the result.
- Larger than the largest size: say it should be split, and suggest the pieces.

## XS

About 10 minutes.

- Reply to a short message when everything needed is at hand.
- Pay a bill, book an appointment.
- Review a small change an agent made on its own.

## S

About an hour.

- A reply that needs some reading first.
- A small, clear fix made by hand, then checked.
- Prepare the agenda and the papers for a meeting.

## M

About half a day.

- Read a few sources and write a two-page summary.
- A change made and tested by hand that an agent could make on its own.
- Plan a trip: travel, a place to stay, the days.

## L

About two days.

- A first draft of a report, or a design for a new feature.
- Move a small website to a new host.
- A tax return, with the papers to gather first.

## XL

A week or more.

- A piece of work with several parts that each take days: plan it as a project, or split it.
